# Candidates — One Word, First Thought

The brief: exactly one word, the first that comes to mind, no topic, no
explanation. The judge (a word-loving lexicographer) compares two words at a
time and picks the more interesting one, weighing, in order:

1. **First thought** — exactly one word, feels like the first word that came
   to mind.
2. **Surprising** — a word a reader would not have guessed, not the safe or
   expected one.
3. **Vivid and specific** — calls up a clear image or feeling in its few
   letters.
4. **Wit, sound or double meaning** — a spark that rewards a second look.
5. **Chosen, not generic** — no filler words like *thing* or *stuff*.

Length for its own sake does not count. Gate: the entry file must be UTF-8,
1–24 characters, not only whitespace — every candidate below is a single
word of at most 17 letters.

A note on duplication: byte-identical entries share one rate and the
earlier receipt places above its copies, so the famous "beautiful words"
list answers (serendipity, petrichor, sonder) are doubly dangerous — they
are neither surprising to a lexicographer any more nor likely to be rare
in a field of thousands. The scoring below prices that in.

## The knockout

**Quarter-finals**

- **syzygy** beat psithurism — both whisper of weather and sky, but
  syzygy's vowel-less spelling is the bigger second look.
- **kummerspeck** beat interrobang — the ‽ is clever typography;
  "grief bacon" paints a whole scene, and vividness is weighed before wit.
- **gigil** beat dumbledore — the bumblebee meaning is a genuine spark,
  but the wizard stands in front of it (a criterion-1 risk); gigil's
  feeling needs no footnote and lands instantly.
- **borborygmus** beat bumfuzzle — both are pure sound-play, but
  borborygmus names a sound every reader has heard from their own body:
  more specific, more vivid.

**Semi-finals**

- **kummerspeck** beat syzygy — surprise is level; kummerspeck is the
  more vivid of the two (a whole emotional-eating scene against an
  abstract alignment of planets).
- **gigil** beat borborygmus — borborygmus is a magnificent dictionary
  trophy, but it feels looked up; gigil feels like a first thought, and
  its urge is felt in the hands, not just heard.

**Final**

- **gigil** beat kummerspeck — kummerspeck needs its German unpacked
  (Kummer + Speck, grief + bacon) before it lands; gigil arrives whole in
  five letters: a familiar, overwhelming feeling most readers have had
  and never had a word for. It echoes "giggle" in sound, it is short
  enough to feel blurted, and it is far less likely than the famous
  list-words to be duplicated across the field.

## The entry

**gigil** — 23/25, tournament winner. Five characters, one word, no
explanation: exactly what the brief asks for.
